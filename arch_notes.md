## Architecture

These are running architecture notes and may be supplanted as progress is made.  For now they are kept here for reference.  Last revised 2026-11-23, on branch `indices`.  Design decisions for internal axes (including deferred matrix-valued fields) are in `axes_design.md`.

Package layout: `lattice.py` and `action.py` are the core; `evolution/` holds Markov-chain algorithms and the `Chain` driver; `stats.py` holds Monte Carlo statistics; `scalar/` holds theory-specific observables; `fermions/` holds gamma matrices and Dirac operators; `linalg.py` holds linear algebra on fields.

### Lattice + LatticeField (`src/lfox/lattice.py`)

- `Lattice(eqx.Module)` holds `st_dims` (spacetime extents, a tuple) and `_dims` (physical dims; may differ from `st_dims` when there is a non-trivial unit cell, as in `HoneycombLattice`).  It carries no JAX arrays, so it can be a static field and compares/hashes by value.  `volume` is the number of sites, including unit-cell structure.
- Concrete lattices implement `shift(field, axis, shift)`. `SquareLattice` uses `jnp.roll` (its red-black `rb_split`/`rb_combine` are stubs); `HoneycombLattice` adds A/B unit-cell handling on axis 0 (note the FIXME there — partially correct).
- `LatticeField(eqx.Module)` carries a `Lattice` (static), the array `F`, boundary conditions `bc` (static, one per spacetime axis, default periodic), and named internal axes `axes` (static tuple of `(name, size)` pairs; `{"spin": 4}` also accepted).  The design spec is the module docstring of `tests/test_lattice_field.py`.
  - **Shape.** `F` is a scalar (a constant field: `F=1` is a unit field) or exactly `dims()` = lattice dims + internal axis sizes; anything else is a `ValueError`.  Integer `F` becomes float.  `.F` is the user-facing view (sites, then internal axes in declared order); storage matches it for now, and only `_aligned` and the hop depend on the layout, so it can change (e.g. internal axes leading) after benchmarking.
  - **Arithmetic** (`_field_op`): fields must share a lattice; internal axes align by *name* -- an axis on one side only broadcasts, disjoint axes give an outer product (left operand's axes first), one name with two sizes is an error.  A raw array must be a scalar or exactly `.F.shape`.
  - **Boundary conditions.** Each entry is a `Phase` -- `lat.phase(n, d)` = exp(i pi n/d), stored as an exact `Fraction` of pi mod 2 so equal BCs hash and compare equal (pytree structure must match exactly through `lax.cond`/`scan`) -- or `lat.DIRICHLET` (spelled 0: no site past the edge).  Spellings 1/-1/0 are normalized at construction; anything else raises.  Under arithmetic the product rule applies (`*`, `/`, integer `**`, `conj` act on phases; Dirichlet absorbs); `+`/`-` need equal BCs; a raw scalar is a periodic constant (adds only to periodic/Dirichlet axes), a raw array is trusted; a non-integer power needs a periodic or Dirichlet field (branch cut).
- `nn_field(axis, shift=1, twist=None)` is the primary kinematic primitive.  Site x gets the value from x - shift; values that wrapped pick up the boundary factor, applied only to the wrapped slab (`shift = qL + r`: bc^-(q+1) on the first r slices, bc^-q on the rest).  `twist` (radians) is an extra traced boundary phase exp(i twist): differentiable, no recompile; not allowed on a Dirichlet axis.  The hot path is `_nn_field`, a JIT-compiled staticmethod with lattice/axis/shift/bc static.
- **Internal-axis operations** (spec: `tests/test_axis_ops.py` docstring; rationale: `axes_design.md`).  None of them ever sums over sites.
  - `lat.einsum(pattern, *operands, axes=None, out_axes=None)`: numpy's einsum per site, with spacetime as the implicit `...`.  Labels are local; a field's labels bind to the axes listed in `axes=` that it has, in that order, and unlisted axes pass through by name.  `axes=None` only for fields with at most one axis.  A label binding two different axis names is an error.  A constant-only output label is named by tracing its constants to field-bound labels (`_reached`), and must reach exactly one name of its size; otherwise `out_axes` names it.  BCs: product rule over field operands.
  - `phi.contract(*names)`, `phi.dot(chi, *names)`, `phi.vdot(chi, *names)` (conjugates `phi`, as `np.vdot`) are thin wrappers over `einsum`.  Default: all (shared) axes, so a term written with `dot` works for any N and any axis name.
  - Repeated axis names (matrix-valued fields) are still an error at construction; their design is recorded but deferred.
- `d()` is the number of spacetime dimensions; `dims()` includes internal axes.
- `LatticeField` is immutable (Equinox); to produce a modified copy, use `copy_new_F(new_F, bc=None)` (`dataclasses.replace`, so shape checks rerun).

### Action (`src/lfox/action.py`)

The module docstring and the docstring of `tests/test_action.py` are the authoritative design statement; this is a summary.

- Two classes, one job each.  **`Term` is what you write**: subclass it and supply `density` (per-site action density, a `LatticeField`) or, for a term too nonlocal to have one, `total` (a scalar).  **`Action` is what you evaluate**: a flat, final sum of terms, built by `Action(*terms)`, `+`, or `sum(...)`.  Never subclass `Action`; a preset theory is a function returning one.  A `Term` has no `S`/`dS`.
- **Fields are named by the hook signature.**  `def density(self, phi)` makes `phi` a *role* of the term, and by default also its external field name.  `rebind` renames: `Term.rebind` is keyed by role, `Action.rebind` by external name (roles are only unique within one term class).  `replicate(n, *names)` is the many-flavor loop (suffixes `_0 .. _{n-1}` to the replicated fields, and to the labels of the terms on them, so copies stay addressable and report separately).
- **Couplings are ordinary dataclass fields**, hence pytree leaves: retuning one (`with_param`) does not retrace, and `jax.grad` can differentiate through it.  Anything that steers Python control flow inside a hook, or is not numeric, must be `eqx.field(static=True)` (checked at construction).  `lambda` is a Python keyword, so the φ⁴ coupling is spelled `lamb` by convention.
- Public API on `Action`: `S_field(fields)`, `S(fields)` (also `action(fields)`), `dS(fields, wrt=None)` and `sample(fields, key)`, all taking/returning **dicts keyed by external field name**.  `S(..., info=True)` / `dS(..., info=True)` also return `{term name: info}`, collected from terms whose `total` (or `gradient`) returns `(value, info)` with `info` a dict (solver diagnostics); reporting terms must have unique names.  `dS` is the gradient dS/dφ, not the force, by autodiff unless a term supplies a hand-written `gradient` hook (such a term is never also differentiated).
- **Sampled vs evolved fields.**  A term may declare `sampled = ('eta',)` and a `draw(key, ...)` hook giving an exact conditional heatbath (pseudofermions).  `Action.sampled_fields` / `evolved_fields` follow; `dS` defaults to the evolved fields.  `sample` gives each drawing term its own key.
- Composition guards: two terms with the same `name` (label, default class name) on the same fields raise `DuplicateTermError`; distinct `label`s declare a deliberate duplicate.  `S[label]` looks up a term.
- Consumers accept a lone term via `eqx.field(converter=Action)`, so downstream code only ever sees an `Action`.
- **A density is one number per site**: a density that still has internal axes raises (`_per_site`, pointing to `dot`/`contract`), as does a `total` that is not a scalar (`_scalar_total`).  Otherwise `phi**4` on an O(N) field would silently be N decoupled quartic fields.

### Evolution (`src/lfox/evolution/`)

Import from the subpackage (`from lfox.evolution import HMC, Chain, LeapfrogIntegrator`), not from the individual modules.

- `base.py` — `Evolver(eqx.Module)` is the abstract Markov transition kernel: `evolve(fields, rng_key) -> (fields, monitor, rng_key)`. **Evolvers are pure.** They carry only what is constant across the chain (the action, algorithm hyperparameters); the fields and RNG key flow through as arguments. Never add mutable state to an `Evolver` — that is what made the old `HMCEvolver` unjittable. The base supplies a concrete `evolve_many` that runs the loop in a `jax.lax.scan`, which stacks whatever `monitor` dict the kernel returns, so the base never needs to know the diagnostic keys.  `as_warmup()` returns the thermalization variant (identity by default).
- `base.py` — `Chain` is the host-side driver: an ordinary stateful Python class (not an `eqx.Module`) owning the RNG key, current fields, saved configurations, diagnostics, and measurements.
  - `measure=[f, (g, freq), ...]`: each measurement function is `f(fields, action) -> {name: value}`, run every trajectory or every `freq`.  `Chain` shape-checks (`jax.eval_shape`) and jit-compiles them at construction, so a function must be traceable (no `float()` on a traced value) and a name returned by two functions is an error up front.
  - Steps in blocks of the GCD of `save_freq` and the measurement frequencies, so it never steps over a trajectory at which something must be recorded; `run(ntraj)` requires `ntraj` to be a multiple of that block.
  - Reading results: `column(name)` (float64 array, leading axis = MC time), `column_traj(name)`, and `columns(*names)` which aligns several on their shared trajectories (what a multi-column jackknife needs).  `acceptance()` and `monitor` hold diagnostics.  `Chain` emits a `RuntimeWarning` when any `"<term>.converged"` monitor entry in a block is false (never a forced rejection).
  - `warmup(n)` uses `evolver.as_warmup()`, records diagnostics only, and advances `warmup_traj`; `traj` counts production trajectories only.
- `hmc.py` — `HMC(Evolver)`. `warmup` is a **static field**, not a call argument ("always accept" is a different transition kernel); build the variant with `as_warmup()`.  Each trajectory first heatbaths the action's sampled fields (if any), then refreshes momenta for the evolved fields only, then integrates and accepts/rejects.  Momenta are `LatticeField`s made by `copy_new_F` (same lattice, axes and BCs as the field they move, so X + εP is defined), and the kinetic density is `0.5 * p.vdot(p)`.  delta_H is computed from **totals**, `H(S, pi) = S + kinetic(pi)`, so nonlocal (total-only) terms run; in float32 that costs precision at large volume (see `fermions_design.md`).  The monitor adds solver info under flat keys `"<term>.<key>"`, stacked in order: S at the start, each force evaluation, S at the proposal (`Nstep + 2` for leapfrog, `2 Nstep + 2` for Omelyan).  The accept/reject uniform must be drawn from an RNG key advanced past *every* momentum draw, or it is a deterministic function of the momenta it is testing; `tests/test_scalar_hmc.py` pins this, and `integration/pseudofermion_marginal.py` checks the heatbath/MD composition against an exact marginal.
- `integrators.py` — `LeapfrogIntegrator`, `OmelyanIntegrator` are `MDIntegrator` subclasses with `eps`, a **static** `Nstep` and a JIT-compiled `_integrate(delta_X, delta_P, X, P) -> (X, P, info)` (behind a thin `integrate` wrapper kept for profiling), a `lax.scan` over steps that stacks the info `delta_P(X, P) -> (dP, info)` returns at each force evaluation.  `X`/`P` are dicts of fields.  `delta_X`/`delta_P` are closures supplied by the evolver — they are passed as static args (`static_argnums=(1,2)`), so re-creating them per call outside an enclosing jit will trigger recompilation.
- Algorithm-specific requirements belong on the `Action`, not on `Evolver`. HMC needs `dS` (free, via autodiff, for any action); exact heatbaths come from `Term.draw`; a cluster update would need bond weights. Keep the `Evolver` interface thin.

### Statistics (`src/lfox/stats.py`)

Host-side NumPy at float64 on *columns* (leading axis = MC time, as from `Chain.column`).

- `jackknife(estimator, *columns, bin_size=1, **constants) -> (estimate, error)`.  `estimator` is the ordinary function of raw samples; positional columns are resampled together (must be aligned); keyword `constants` pass through.  Deletes blocks of *raw* samples rather than binning first, which keeps nonlinear moments right.  Cost is N / `bin_size` + 1 evaluations; `bin_size` is the lever against τ_int, and `bin_size=1` is right for data already binned (bin the primary observables, not the field a nonlinear estimator consumes).
- `tau_int(series) -> TauInt` with Sokal's automatic window and Madras–Sokal error.  `TauInt(tau, error, window, resolved, gamma)` prints as `TauInt(tau=4.52(18), window=23)`; an unresolved window prints `tau>=` and is only a lower bound.

### Scalar observables (`src/lfox/scalar/`)

- Two kinds, deliberately separate.  **Per-configuration** observables take a `LatticeField` and are jittable — what a `Chain` measurement function returns: `magnetization` (m = M/V; m_a for O(N)), `nn_product`, `structure_factor(phi, n)` (G(p), not connected), `correlator(phi, axis)` (zero-momentum C(t), not connected).  **Ensemble** estimators take columns and are nonlinear in averages, so their errors come from `jackknife`: `susceptibility(m, volume)`, `binder`, `xi_2nd(m, Gp, volume, L)`, `effective_mass(C)`.
- Broken-phase χ is `susceptibility(np.abs(m), volume)`; for O(N), `np.linalg.norm(m, axis=-1)`.
- Ensemble estimators return a quiet `nan` where the quantity does not exist (`xi_2nd` outside χ ≥ ⟨G(p_min)⟩ > 0; `effective_mass` at t = 0 and where noise pushes the cosh ratio below 1), rather than `inf` or a warning per jackknife deletion.
- **O(N) extends this module**; the plain scalar is N = 1.  Internal axes trail in `magnetization`; G(p), C(t) and `nn_product` sum over components; estimators contract the trailing axes of their columns, so χ = V(⟨m·m⟩ − ⟨m⟩·⟨m⟩) (summed over components, no 1/N) and Binder U = 1 − N/(N+2)·⟨(m·m)²⟩/⟨m·m⟩² (N from the column shape).  V is always `lattice.volume`, never `F.size`.  The nonlinear sigma model waits for the manifold field-type protocol.

### Fermions (`src/lfox/fermions/`) and linear algebra (`src/lfox/linalg.py`)

Rewritten on branch `fermions`; the design, and the order of the remaining work (pseudofermions, after CG with diagnostics and the diagnostics plumbing into the HMC monitor), is in `fermions_design.md`.  The legacy `Dirac4DFermionField`, `WilsonDiracOp` and `MRSolver` are deleted.

- A spinor is a plain `LatticeField` with a `"spin"` axis (usually complex, antiperiodic in time); there is no fermion subclass.
- `gamma.py`: `gammas(d)` (shape `(d, s, s)`, `gammas(d)[mu]` goes with lattice axis mu): Pauli in d = 2, 3, DeGrand–Rossi in d = 4.  `gamma5(d)` for even d: σ3 = −iγ0γ1 in d = 2, γ0γ1γ2γ3 = diag(1, 1, −1, −1) in d = 4.
- `dirac.py`: `wilson(psi, kappa)`, hopping form ψ − κ Σ_μ [(1 − γ_μ) ψ(x+μ̂) + (1 + γ_μ) ψ(x−μ̂)], acting on the spin axis via `lat.einsum`; other axes pass through.  ψ(x+μ̂) is `psi.nn_field(mu, -1)`.  Operators are plain linear functions of fields, closed over their background (`lambda psi: wilson(psi, kappa) + g * sigma * psi`).
- `lfox.linalg`: `dense(A, like)` (the matrix in `.F.ravel()` order, for small lattices) and `dagger(A, like)` (the adjoint of any linear operator, by `jax.linear_transpose` and conjugation; it does not assume γ5-hermiticity, which a pseudoscalar coupling breaks).
- `lfox.linalg.cg(A, b, x0=None, tol=1e-10, maxiter=1000) -> (x, info)`: CG for Hermitian positive-definite A, `info = {"iterations", "residual", "converged"}` (relative residual; stops at `maxiter` without raising).  A `lax.while_loop` inside `lax.custom_linear_solve(..., transpose_solve=solve, has_aux=True)`: jittable, and differentiated implicitly (one extra solve in the backward pass, whose diagnostics JAX discards).  `symmetric=True` would be wrong for complex Hermitian A (Aᵀ = conj(A)) and gives a silently wrong gradient; `tests/test_linalg.py` pins this against finite differences.
- Gotcha met while writing `wilson`: a sum of fields must not start from `0` (or use `sum()`), because a constant cannot be added to an antiperiodic field.

### Tests and integration

- `tests/phi4.py` — `ScalarTerm`, the reference O(N) φ⁴ term (written with `dot`; a field with no axes is N = 1), with an analytic gradient for checking autodiff.  `tests/test_on_model.py` pins it as the O(N) model: N = 1 reduction, rotation invariance, force, ⟨e^{−ΔH}⟩ = 1, and free-field equipartition and χ under HMC.  `tests/conftest.py` provides fixtures (`scalar_action`, `lat4`, `phi4`).
- `tests/free_field.py` — exact, independent samples of the free (λ = 0) field via FFT (`sample_on` for N independent fields), plus closed-form χ, ξ₂, C(t) and pole mass: the reference values the observables are tested against.
- `integration/reproduce_schaefer.py` — reproduction of Schaefer's φ⁴ exercises; `integration/pseudofermion_marginal.py` — heatbath + HMC composition against an exact marginal.  Both are jupytext-paired notebooks.

## Conventions / things to know

- Everything performance-critical is `jax.jit`-compiled.  Modules (`Action`, `Evolver`, integrators) are passed to jitted methods as pytrees: numeric fields (couplings, `eps`) are traced leaves, and `eqx.field(static=True)` fields go into the compilation key.  Changing a static field retraces; changing a coupling does not.  Adding mutable state to these `eqx.Module`s will break this.
- `static_argnums` is still used for genuinely static arguments: lattice/axis/shift in `_nn_field`, the closures passed to `_integrate`, the field tuple in `dS`, `traj` in `evolve_many`.
- Boundary conditions: psi(x + L) = bc * psi(x), so a value that wraps backwards picks up bc^-1 (signs show up at the target site).  See the `LatticeField` notes above.
- Field arithmetic and `nn_field` always return new `LatticeField` instances; never mutate `self.F` — `LatticeField` is a frozen `eqx.Module` and assignment raises.
- Field collections at every public boundary are dicts keyed by external field name; only author hooks see roles.
- The pure/stateful boundary is the jit boundary. `Lattice`, `LatticeField`, `Term`, `Action`, `MDIntegrator` and `Evolver` are pure `eqx.Module`s; `Chain` is the one stateful class, and it lives entirely outside jit.  Statistics (`lfox.stats`, ensemble estimators) are host-side NumPy, after the chain.
- Enable `jax_enable_x64` for physics runs; the tests and examples do.
